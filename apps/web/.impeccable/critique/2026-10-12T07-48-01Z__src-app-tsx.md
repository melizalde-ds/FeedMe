Target: `/login`, `/register` and the Setup, Plan and Grocery steps on `/`.

Evidence: source in `apps/web/src`, 68 baseline screenshots in `.redesign/before/`, the CLI detector, and the detector overlay run in the live Setup, Plan and Grocery pages.

## Design specificity verdict

**Review.** The page could belong to any meal planner: a cream ground, a green header, an orange accent, rounded white cards, a hero with a logo and a script headline. The parts that are specific to FeedMe (allergies first and locked once saved, three checks per meal, real prices or none, an agent that shops) are stated in sentences and never shown. The allergy block is a pink box. The safety check is the word "OK".

**Detector.** CLI scan of `src`: 3 findings, all the side-tab accent border (`index.css` 687, 724, 732). In the live pages: 9 on Setup (6 cards inside cards, a 133-character line, the side-tab, the cream palette), 23 on Plan (21 placeholders at 4.4:1 against the 4.5:1 floor, the side-tab, the cream palette), 7 on Grocery (4 cards inside cards, 3 side-tabs, a 142-character line, the cream palette). The detector agrees with the review on the cream palette, the nested cards and the side-tabs, and caught the placeholder contrast, which the review missed. No false positives.

## Overall impression

The logic is careful and the copy is honest. The surface hides both. The biggest opportunity is to let the product's own facts (what the household avoids, what each meal was checked for, what the numbers are and whose they are) carry the design, in type people can read.

## What is working

- **The words.** Errors name the problem and the next step. Estimates are called estimates. Missing figures stay missing.
- **States are all there.** Loading, empty, error, partial and success exist for every surface, and the wait says what is happening.
- **Semantics.** Real tables with captions and scoped headers, labelled inputs, `role="alert"` and `role="status"`, a 3px focus ring, Escape on dialogs.

## Priority issues

**[P1] Type that is hard to read.** Every heading is Great Vibes, a wedding script. Hints, table cells, recipe metadata and the fine print are Bad Script, a handwriting face, at 13 to 15px. That includes the "Used in" column of the grocery list, the calories line in the Check column and the note that the numbers are not verified. Body text is the system font and form legends are Lora: four voices. The text a person with an allergy most needs is the least legible on the page. *Fix:* one workhorse family with real weights, a fixed scale, and tabular figures for numbers.

**[P1] The plan grid is a wall of controls.** 21 dropdowns, 21 number boxes and up to 21 "Recipe" buttons at equal weight. Meal names are cut off inside the dropdowns ("Tortellini Tomato Spinach S"). At 390px the table scrolls sideways, so Lunch, Dinner and the Check column are off screen, and Check is the safety information. *Fix:* show the meal's name in full, move the rare controls (people per meal) off the grid, and give phones a layout of their own.

**[P1] Two design systems glued together.** `index.css` holds BEM classes, and newer screens use Tailwind strings from `ui/styles.js`. A page mixes both: three button families, two input styles, panels inside panels inside fieldsets. The forced dark theme sets `--green` to a different value than the system dark theme, so the header changes colour depending on how dark mode was reached. *Fix:* one token set, one component vocabulary.

**[P2] Setup is one long form with no rhythm.** The hero and logo take the first screen every visit. Three fieldsets of very different length sit in equal columns, leaving a column of dead space under "Household". "Save to my account", "Generate meal plan" and "Reset to defaults" sit together at similar weight. *Fix:* order by consequence (allergies, then the household, then goals), one primary action, and no hero for a signed-in user.

**[P2] Grocery buries its best part.** The list is 61 rows, 59 of them with a dash for the amount, and the buying agent, the most distinctive thing in the product, starts five screens down. Its states arrive as a stack of similar banners. *Fix:* put the agent beside the list, and make the list scannable (grouping, and no column of dashes).

**[P3] A dialog for everything.** Reading a recipe, writing one and confirming a run all open the same centred modal, including a form that is nine screens long on a phone.

## Persona red flags

**Sam, keyboard and screen reader, low vision.** Script and handwriting faces at small sizes. 21 placeholders under the contrast floor. On a phone the Check column is off screen with no hint that the table scrolls. Focus rings, labels and roles are good.

**Casey, on a phone.** The plan table needs sideways scrolling for every row. "Remake the plan" sits above the fold and "Confirm this week" is far below it. Tap targets on "Recipe" are 30px tall.

**Jordan, first visit.** Setup opens with 10 allergens, 8 diets, 4 macro fields, 2 term lists, a budget, a time and a period, all at once. "Recalculate carbs and fat" can leave the form unable to submit, with a browser tooltip as the only explanation.

**The household's cook, planning on Sunday** (from PRODUCT.md). Wants the week at a glance and gets truncated names. Wants to know each meal is safe and gets "OK" in green, followed by "below your calorie goal, short on protein" on all five days with nothing to do about it.

## Minor observations

- A day's protein can read "27.200000000000003 g".
- The logo is a 331 kB PNG used at 150 by 40.
- The week tabs use `role="tab"` with no tab panel.
- "Heads up" and "What changed" look the same apart from their tint.
- The JS bundle is 588 kB in one chunk.

## Questions to consider

- What would this look like if the allergy statement were the most confident thing on the page?
- Does the week need 21 dropdowns, or 21 meals you can read?
- Why is the agent that shops for you at the bottom of a list?
